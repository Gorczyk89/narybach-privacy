Na rybach – fishing logbook

Privacy Policy

Effective 27 September 2026

  • I collect no data about you. Na rybach has no accounts, no server, no ads, no analytics and no tracking.
  • Your logbook, photos and spots are only on your iPhone.
  • For the weather, the app connects straight to public services and sends them only an approximate place. It sends a spot’s exact area only when you download its aerial photo yourself.

Who is responsible for the app

Na rybach is made by Rafał Gorczyński, a private person in Poland. Contact: rafal.gorczynski89@gmail.com.

What stays on your iPhone

Everything you save in the app: trips, catches, photos, voice notes, spots with their points and parking, rules, the documents in the angler’s wallet, plans and settings, as well as downloaded forecasts and aerial photos. I have no access to them, and the app sends nothing from them except what is described below.

  • Photos are saved without their location or other camera metadata. In “Catches from Photos” the app reads the date and place of the photo you pick, then drops the metadata.
  • Location (if you allow it) is used to save where you caught a fish, fetch the weather, suggest the spot and remind you at a spot. The reminders work within the iPhone, without a server.
  • The camera, the microphone, the barometer (Motion & Fitness) and saving to Photos work only on the iPhone: photos, measuring a fish, spot codes, voice notes, the pressure during a trip and cards saved to Photos.
  • The app doesn’t sync the logbook with any cloud. If your iPhone’s backup is on (iCloud or a computer), iOS may include Na rybach’s data in it: that’s a system setting.
  • Backups, the CSV export, the catch register (PDF) and automatic backups go where you save them (e.g. Files or iCloud Drive), and that place’s rules apply there. You can encrypt backups with a password; the password is only in this iPhone’s keychain.

Who the app connects to and what it sends

Only the services below, straight from your iPhone, with no server of mine in between. Like any internet connection, a request carries your connection’s IP address and the name and version of the app and the system; it carries no identifier of you or the phone. The app keeps no cookies between connections.

MET Norway – the Norwegian Meteorological Institute (api.met.no)

  • What for: the weather at a catch and at the start of a trip, “Today at the Water”, “When to Go” and other forecasts. Only while the app is open.
  • What it gets: coordinates rounded to two decimal places (about 1 km), never a spot’s exact place.
  • What it keeps: MET’s terms say the IP address and the coordinates of requests are stored in its logs in Oslo (Norway); MET’s privacy policy says it keeps IP addresses for up to 90 days.

MET’s terms · MET’s privacy policy

NASA POWER – NASA (USA)

  • What for: archived weather for catches from about 3 days ago and older (also from old photos, from 2001), and filling in pressure changes.
  • What it gets: only the centre of a cell of its grid (0.5° × 0.625°, about 55 × 43 km) and a range of dates.
  • What it keeps: NASA’s privacy policy lists the IP address among the information collected automatically; raw logs are kept temporarily.

NASA’s privacy policy

Geoportal – the Polish Head Office of Geodesy and Cartography (GUGiK)

  • What for: the spot’s aerial photo (orthophoto) for use without signal, for spots in Poland.
  • When: only when you tap “Download Aerial Photo” (or download it again).
  • What it gets: the exact area around the spot: a square at least 600 m across, covering the spot, its points and the parking.
  • What it keeps: Geoportal’s privacy policy describes only its website’s cookies. The service sets technical cookies; the app discards them after each download.

Apple Maps (Apple)

  • The maps in the app are Apple’s (MapKit): to show them, Apple gets the area you’re looking at.
  • “Directions” and “Directions to Parking” open the Maps app with the exact place as the destination.
  • “Share an Apple Maps Link” makes a link with the spot’s exact place; whoever you send it to gets it.

Apple’s privacy policy

App Store (Apple): Plus purchases

  • Purchases, “Restore Purchases” and “Redeem Code” are handled by the App Store. I get no payment details and not your Apple Account. The app gets only Apple’s signed confirmations of what’s bought, kept on this iPhone.
  • Apple gives me aggregate statistics (e.g. the number of downloads and sales) and crash reports, the latter only from people who agreed in their iPhone’s settings to share them with app developers. Apple doesn’t tell me who you are in them.

IMGW-PIB: only when you tap “Check the water level”

  • The button opens IMGW’s public map (hydro.imgw.pl) in Safari at the spot’s approximate place (two decimal places, about 1 km).
  • The place is in the part of the address after “#”, which the browser doesn’t send to the server, but the page’s script reads it to show the map. The page uses Google Analytics.
  • From then on, IMGW’s terms and privacy policy apply. The app takes no data from IMGW.

Other sites, and what you send yourself

  • Links in the app (data sources, the guide, this page) open in Safari; those sites’ rules apply there.
  • Catch, trip and season cards, spot files and QR codes, backups and plans added to the Calendar go only where you send or save them. The cards don’t show where a fish was caught.

Legal basis (GDPR)

  • The connections described above (weather from MET Norway and NASA POWER, the aerial photo from GUGiK, Apple's maps) are needed for the features you use in the app: Article 6(1)(b) GDPR. The app sends only what they need: your connection's IP address and an approximate place.
  • NASA is in the United States. The request to NASA POWER, and with it the IP address and the centre of the grid cell, goes there because it is necessary to fetch the archived weather you ask for: Article 49(1)(b) GDPR. MET Norway (Norway) and GUGiK (Poland) are in the European Economic Area.
  • If you write to me, I use your address and message to reply, based on legitimate interest: Article 6(1)(f) GDPR. I keep them no longer than needed to deal with the matter.
  • Purchases are handled by Apple as a separate controller, under its own privacy policy.

What the app doesn’t do

  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking and no other companies’ libraries (SDKs).
  • It doesn’t sell or pass your data to anyone, and doesn’t combine it with data from other apps or websites.
  • It creates no account and doesn’t ask for your name, e-mail or phone number.

Children

The app collects no data from anyone, children included. A child’s purchase of Plus in Family Sharing may need a parent’s approval (“Ask to Buy”).

Your data and deleting it

  • I don’t have your data, so there is nothing for me to hand over or delete at your request.
  • You delete entries in the app. Deleting the app from your iPhone deletes the logbook and everything the app saved on it. iOS may keep the backup password in the keychain after the app is deleted: remove it first in Settings → Your Data → Backup Password.
  • Backups and exports you made are deleted where you saved them.
  • You can turn off access to location, the camera, the microphone and motion in the iPhone’s Settings; the app works without them too.
  • For data in the logs of MET, NASA, GUGiK and Apple, ask them under their privacy policies.
